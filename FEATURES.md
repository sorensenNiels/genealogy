# Features

What the genealogy app can do today (✅). Planned work (🚧) and undecided ideas (💡) are in [IDEAS.md](IDEAS.md). Entries are kept short by design — the _why_ behind a non-obvious decision lives in [TECH_STACK.md](TECH_STACK.md), not here; this file is a scannable "what exists" list.

## Core record-keeping

- ✅ Person records: given/last names, nicknames, title/suffix, gender, living/deceased flag, a human-readable public id (e.g. `LZ4Y-9K2`), computed age, notes.
- ✅ Alternate names (maiden, married, spelling variants) — add, edit, remove, each with its own note.
- ✅ Life events, birth/death included (christening, burial, immigration, emigration, naturalization, residence, occupation, other, ...), each with a date, place, markdown notes, and structured per-type detail fields (ship & port, employer & job title, cemetery & plot, ...). Sorted chronologically on the person page.
- ✅ Marriages/unions: status (married/divorced/partnership/annulled/widowed), start/end dates and places, more than one per pair over time.
- ✅ Relationships: parents, spouses, children, with parentage type (birth/adopted/step/foster/unknown).
- ✅ Sources & citations, on a person or on a specific fact (a name, event, or union), with a confidence level, page/volume/entry ID, and a note — citable directly from a fact's own edit dialog.
- ✅ Unified, forgiving date entry everywhere ("abt 1854", "BET 1900 AND 1905", ...) with a live preview and one normalized display format across the app.
- ✅ Overview (the landing page) starts with you: "Your place in the tree" shows the person you've chosen as yourself, how many ancestors are known in each generation back to your 3rd great-grandparents ("6 of 62 ancestors known in 5 generations") and how far back your line goes, with buttons to open the tree on you and to list your ancestors; without a linked person it asks you to choose one. "Continue where you left off" links back to the tree as you left it (centre, chart, direction) and lists the last eight people you opened.
- ✅ Overview cards: Settings → Overview chooses which cards show and in what order for everyone; each member can hide cards for themselves from the Overview's Customize menu, and drag the cards into their own order with Arrange (mouse, touch or keyboard; "Use the site's order" goes back) — both saved on their profile — but not bring back a card the site hides.
- ✅ Overview cards for the family (everyone): "On this day" — births, baptisms, marriages, deaths and burials on today's date in past years, exact dates only, deceased people only unless Settings → Overview lets editors see living birthdays; "Featured ancestor" — a deceased person a day, from your own ancestors when you've linked yourself, with photo, how they're related to you and their life summary; "The family at a glance" — people, birth span, surnames, birthplaces, sources, photos and the earliest-born person, linking into the People list; "Where the family came from" — the most common birthplaces. For editors: "Research to-do" (open research notes, and counts of people missing each checklist item, linking to the People list's Missing filter), "Census gaps" (the censuses with the most people alive then and not yet found or searched) and "Recently added and changed". For admins: "Things to look at" (likely and possible duplicates, data-integrity findings, presumed deceased, unlinked photo faces, pending invitations, each linking to its tool) and "System status" (backend and Immich). Settings → Overview chooses which events "On this day" lists.
- ✅ People list: avatar, gender icon, living/deceased badge, age column, pagination. Name search matches every word against first and last names, alternate names included (a row found by one says "also recorded as …"); a "Similar spellings" box also finds names that sound the same — Kristensen finds Christensen, Sorensen finds Sørensen, Sørensen finds Sørensdatter. Sort by clicking a column header (name, age, birth, death, place), or from a menu in the card view. Filters for place, birth range, gender, "Missing", "My ancestors" and (editors) "Open research notes". Search, filters, sort and page are in the URL, so they survive Back, a reload and a shared link. Settings → People list (admin) sets the default sort, the default view (table or cards) and whether the Born/Died columns show full dates or years; each member's own table/card choice is saved on their profile and follows them between devices. Rows can be one line or two: the second line names the parents and spouses ("Son of Jens Pedersen & Ane Nielsdatter · ⚭ 1874 Maren Hansdatter") and puts the birth and death places under the dates — what tells people with the same name apart. Each member switches between one and two lines (saved on their profile); Settings → People list sets the default and what the second line holds. Rows can be grouped by last name, birth century or birth parish, under collapsible headers that give each group's full size even when it runs on to the next page. A Columns menu shows or hides any column but the name, saved per member; Settings → People list sets the default grouping and columns.
- ✅ Research checklist: the "research completeness" bar on the person page opens a checklist of what's recorded and what's missing — birth date and place, both parents, a source for the birth, death date, place and source (skipped for the living), a photo — and names the next thing to research. The People list's "Missing" filter finds everyone still missing any one item.
- ✅ Full-text search (`/search`, and a search box in the header): notes, citations, life events and places, marriages, witness/godparent roles, research notes, and records with the people in them — not names, which the People list searches. Every word matches as a prefix, matches are highlighted, results can be narrowed to people, citations, records or research notes. Viewers get no hits on living people. See "Full-text search" in TECH_STACK.md.
- ✅ Person profile photo (local upload, or picked from an Immich photo — see Photos below).
- ✅ Delete a person outright, once they have no recorded parent/spouse/child; confirm-before-remove on relationships, alternate names, and life events.
- ✅ Merge a duplicate into a person ("Merge with…"): a preview of what moves, everything moved in one transaction, nothing on the kept person overwritten, identical facts not doubled; refused for people related to each other.
- ✅ Duplicate person detection (Tools → Possible duplicates): pairs that look like one person recorded twice, rated Likely/Possible/Name only with the reasons (name, dates, places, parents, spouse). Danish names compare by sound — Søren/Sören, Christensen/Kristensen, Petersen/Pedersen, Sørensen/Sørensdatter — and alternate names count. Merge a pair from there (choosing which to keep), or mark it "Not the same person" so it isn't suggested again. "Merge with…" on the person page lists that person's likely duplicates first. See "Duplicate detection" in TECH_STACK.md.
- ✅ Research notes per person: open questions to chase down later (a "brick wall"), each with a type and a resolved flag.
- ✅ Single-person printable report ("family group sheet") at `/people/$personId/print`.

## Person page

- ✅ Personal details opens as "life at a glance": a generated summary ("Born 3 Mar 1850 in Ribe. Married Karen Hansdatter (12 Oct 1874). Had 6 children. Died 1 Jun 1921 in Esbjerg, aged 71."), birth, unions and death as milestones on a line with places, partners and ages, the other names the person went by, and — for editors — one-click prompts for missing facts. An Edit button swaps in the form (one Save/Cancel for all of it) and back; Viewers only ever get the read view, and a Settings option opens the form straight away instead.
- ✅ Family events in the Life events timeline: children's births and deaths, a spouse's death and the parents' deaths, shown faintly within the person's own lifetime, each linking to the relative. On by default; a Settings option.
- ✅ Presumed deceased: someone still marked living who was born more than N years ago (110 by default, set on the Settings page; 0 turns it off) gets a "probably deceased — mark as deceased" hint in Personal details, a Tools page check marks everyone it applies to in one confirmed step, and the GEDCOM import uses the same N.
- ✅ Consistency warnings: facts that can't all be true — death before birth, burial before death, an event or marriage before birth or after burial, a marriage too young, a parent too young or a mother too old at a birth, a child born after the mother died or more than a year after the father died. Shown to editors at the top of the person page (checked against birth parents and birth children) and listed in Tools → Data integrity, counted in the Overview's "Things to look at". Vague dates get the benefit of the doubt: only what no reading of the dates allows is flagged. The limits (youngest parent 12, oldest mother 55, youngest to marry 14; 0 turns one off) are set in Settings → Person; a living person past the "presumed deceased" age is the existing "probably deceased" hint.
- ✅ Settings → Person page chooses which cards a person's page shows and their order within each column (Personal details and Family always show); how names read everywhere in the app ("Anders Jensen", "JENSEN, Anders" or "Anders JENSEN"); full dates or years only on the person page, and whether ages show; and the Family card's scope — half-siblings (marked "half", on by default), step-parents, step-siblings and stepchildren, and parents-, siblings- and children-in-law, worked out from the marriages recorded.

## Records

- ✅ Records entered as written — census households and church book entries (baptism, marriage, burial): what, when, parish, address/church/cemetery, household or entry number, source with page and a link to the scan, and one row per person named, with their role (child, father, godparent, groom, witness, deceased, informant, … or a census household position) and the as-written age, sex, marital status, occupation, residence and birthplace. Each row can be linked to someone in the tree (candidates show whether their birth fits the age) or used to create them. Saving creates what the record implies — a census, christening or burial event for the principals, a marriage between the couple (reusing one already recorded), godparents/witnesses/informants/officiants as associations — each citing the source, kept in step on every edit; deleting the record removes them.
- ✅ Censuses from around the world: built-in censuses for Denmark, Norway, Sweden, England & Wales, Scotland, the USA and Canada — picking one fills in the census day and shows only the columns that census had — plus custom censuses for anywhere else, added in Settings.
- ✅ Family links from records: a record suggests the parent and spouse links it implies (a baptism's parents, a census household's head, spouse and children) that the tree doesn't have yet, one click each — or, per a Settings choice, makes them automatically on save.
- ✅ On the person page, a Records card: census coverage — every census during their life, for the chosen countries and any they already appear in, as found / searched but not found / not yet searched (a research to-do list) — every record they appear in, an "Add record" menu, and — where the person record lacks them — the birth estimated from a recorded age and the recorded birthplace, one click to use. The data integrity check flags record ages that don't fit the recorded birth.
- ✅ Records settings: which record types are offered, which countries' censuses, which countries the coverage grid lists, custom censuses, the confidence of the citations records create, and suggested vs. automatic family links.

## Tree & visualization

- ✅ Tree view (`/tree`): ancestors and descendants around one person, expandable generation-by-generation, add a relative (parent, spouse, child or sibling) directly from the tree, a docked detail panel instead of navigating away. Each card says how the person is related to the centre person ("Grandfather", "2nd cousin once removed", in Danish "Farfar", "Moster", "Næstsøskendebarn"), and the line between partners shows their marriage year ("⚭ 1874"). Display settings for the tree on the Settings page (admin, site-wide): photos or initials only, gender badge, mourning band for the deceased, relationship labels, marriage years, years or full dates, highlighting the direct line, and which chart and direction the tree opens with. Drawn with topola: relatives (the default — ancestors, descendants, siblings, aunts/uncles, cousins), ancestors or descendants charts, top-down or left to right, with pan/zoom; click a person for the panel, double-click to centre on them. Getting around a big tree: re-centring glides to the new person instead of reloading (the cards already loaded stay and rearrange), "Find in tree" pans to anyone loaded, a minimap shows where you are and can be dragged, and the chart works from the keyboard (arrow keys between people, Enter to centre, Home, +/−/0 to zoom; the selected person is announced to screen readers). A "+" at the bottom of the selected (or hovered) card opens a menu to add a father, mother, sibling or child — editors only, offering only what wouldn't make a duplicate, and "load first" where the tree hasn't looked yet. Doing things from the tree: a right-click menu on a card (or the menu key / Shift+F10 on the selected one) to centre there, open the profile, load parents/children, and — for editors — add a father, mother, sibling, spouse or child; export the whole tree as PNG, PDF or SVG with photos embedded; and the tree reopens where each member left it (centre person, chart and direction on their profile, so on any device; zoom per browser). Settings for the minimap, animations (always off under reduced motion), remembering where each member was, and whether the tree opens fitted or at 100% on the centre person. See "Tree view: topola" in TECH_STACK.md.
- ✅ Show both sides, ancestors only, or descendants only (a toggle on the canvas, kept in the URL).
- ✅ Remove a parent, spouse or child relationship from the tree's side panel (confirm first; people and marriage records are kept — adding the same spouse back reuses the kept marriage rather than creating a second).

## Photos

- ✅ Family photo library via [Immich](https://immich.app) (self-hosted on its own server): albums, upload, add-existing-photos, a queued "download all" (zipped, notified when ready — see below), face detection/clustering linked to genealogy people, tag/unlink a face from the photo viewer, a person's own Photos card. See "Photos & albums: Immich" in TECH_STACK.md.
- ✅ Immich is optional: when it isn't configured or can't be reached, every photo and album feature is turned off (no Photos menu item, no Photos card), and the Overview page shows Immich's status and why it's off.
- ✅ Set a person's profile photo from an existing Immich photo, with a circular crop.
- ✅ Local album metadata (date, place) plus a filters panel (name/kind/place/date range/missing-info) and sort, for cutting through a large album collection.
- ✅ Physical albums (a real scanned album: grows by upload, with a cover color/style, where-found and scan notes) and virtual albums (curated collections of photos that already live in a physical album, showing their source).
- ✅ Album categories (e.g. family names): a Categories list in the Photos sidebar with counts, albums grouped under category headings (one shelf per category in book view), an album can be in several categories, and categories can be picked or created from the New/Edit album dialogs.
- ✅ Photo book reading mode (N photos per page, paper styles), manual photo order, captions and Immich tags per photo.
- ✅ Delete albums and photos (photos go to Immich's trash, recoverable there), and remove a photo from an album.

## Import / export

- ✅ GEDCOM export from the Tools page, as 5.5.1 (default, most compatible) or 7.0: every person, name, event, family, source, repository and citation; notes as plain text for other programs; this app's own fields travel as underscore tags so export → import is lossless. See "GEDCOM export" in TECH_STACK.md.
- ✅ GEDCOM import from the Tools page: pick a file, see a preview (what will be added, anything that can't be imported, and incoming people who share a name with someone already in the tree), then import. Everyone is added as a new person — likely duplicates are reported, then merged by hand ("Merge with…" on the person page). Reads UTF-8, UTF-16, Windows-1252 and ANSEL files, Julian dates and double years (`1749/50`), and the common GEDCOM 7 forms. Media is reported, not imported (a 5.5.1 file only holds file paths). See "GEDCOM import" in TECH_STACK.md.
- ✅ Witnesses, godparents and other roles on a person's records (a "Witnesses & godparents" card; tied to an event, a marriage, or neither; the person can be someone in the tree or just a name), GEDCOM privacy restrictions (RESN — kept and exported back, but not shown or enforced: the living are always private, the deceased never restricted), other programs' research to-dos imported as research notes, and place coordinates (PostGIS, no map UI yet), repositories with contact details and call numbers, and external IDs (FamilySearch, Ancestry, REFN, EXID — shown on an "External IDs" card, FamilySearch linked to its profile) — all imported from and exported back to GEDCOM.

## Sharing & collaboration

- ✅ Sign-in by email/password or Google; joining is by invitation only (the very first account on a new install becomes admin).
- ✅ Members & roles: Admin (members, Settings, Tools, merge/delete), Editor (edits the tree and photos), Viewer (read-only). A Members page to change roles, remove members, and manage invitations.
- ✅ Invite family members by email: a link valid 14 days, with role, an optional "this is you" person and a personal message; sent in the inviting admin's language, and always shown to copy in case email isn't set up.
- ✅ Living people are shown to Viewers by name only — dates, places, notes, photos and records hidden; the deceased are never restricted; a Viewer's own person is never hidden from them. Settings → Person page can show the birth year too, or nothing: "Living person" in the tree and Family card, and left out of the People list. What a Viewer isn't shown doesn't steer what they do see either: the People list's date, place and "Missing" filters leave living people out, and sorting or grouping by birth, death, age or place treats their hidden facts as unknown.
- ✅ Static family website: Tools → Family website exports the whole tree as a zip of plain pages — everyone A–Z with a filter box and surname index, a page per person (names, vitals, family links, life events, notes, footnoted sources), a sources page, and the interactive tree (the app's own chart: pan/zoom, minimap, keyboard, chart and direction switcher). Opens offline from `index.html` or on any web server; English or Danish; the family site's theme, light and dark. Living people are name only — the Viewer rule, the exporting admin included. Profile photos only. See "Static family website" in TECH_STACK.md.

## Tools

An admin utility page (`/tools`).

- ✅ Backup the database (background job, notified when ready) and restore from a backup file (locked against overlap, typed confirmation). Alongside it, "Download all albums" queues a zip of every photo in every album the same way — one notification per file if the library needs more than one archive.
- ✅ Re-scan for faces (Immich), GEDCOM export and import.
- ✅ Data integrity checks (dangling citations, dangling Immich links, record ages that disagree with a birth) and a two-store consistency check (Postgres vs. the Apache AGE graph).
- ✅ Development tools, shown only when the app runs on localhost: the component showcase (every shared UI component in both themes).

## Platform

- ✅ Dark mode.
- ✅ Themes: five built in (Nova, Heritage, Fjord, Meadow, Ink) — colors, heading and body fonts, corner roundness. Admins pick the family site's theme and make their own on Settings → Appearance (fonts from a bundled set, every color for light and dark, live preview); each member can choose a different one on their profile.
- ✅ A shared component library: shadcn/ui on Base UI, composed into app-level components (React Aria only for the photo grid, file drop zone, tag chips and calendar).

## Internationalization

- ✅ English/Danish language switching (the language menu in the header, left of the user menu), saved to the account and mirrored to a cookie for fast/SSR reads. Covers full scope, not just UI strings:
  - Every user-facing string across `apps/frontend/src/routes/` (all person, photos, tree, tools, settings, profile pages) via `i18next`/`react-i18next` — a fresh instance per SSR request, avoiding the cross-request-leak class of bug documented in `trpc-proxy-client.ts`.
  - Backend error messages translated centrally in the frontend layer, via one custom tRPC link (`apps/frontend/src/server/trpc-error-translation-link.ts`) rather than touching any of the ~40 server function files — the NestJS backend stays English-only by design.
  - Danish free-text date entry ("ca. 1854", "før 1920", "mellem 1900 og 1905") — real parser support in `packages/core/src/historical-date.ts`, with its own Danish quality-word/month-name tables, not just translated labels.
  - Out of scope for this pass: `packages/ui`'s own internal default copy (e.g. a component's default confirm-dialog text), configurable-options values (user-authored data, not app strings), better-auth's own auth-flow error messages, and the dev-only `/dev/components` showcase page.
